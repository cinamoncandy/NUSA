# NUSA Investment Knowledge & Financial Reasoning Principle

## Status

This document is a normative NUSA architecture principle. It defines the minimum investment knowledge, financial reasoning, provenance, validation, and learning boundaries required for NUSA investment intelligence.

It is subordinate to `docs/NUSA_CORE_ARCHITECTURE_PRINCIPLE.md` and must be interpreted consistently with `docs/NUSA_AI_ARCHITECTURE_V1.md` and the NUSA Safety Constitution.

## Principle

NUSA must not begin as a financially blank AI that learns investment fundamentals only from its own trading history.

NUSA starts with governed foundational investment knowledge and then improves through current market knowledge, research, validation, and learned experience.

The permanent rule is:

> Foundational knowledge provides the financial grammar; market knowledge provides the current context; learned knowledge provides evidence-backed adaptation. Learned experience must never silently rewrite foundational financial or safety invariants.

## 1. Knowledge Classes

NUSA separates investment knowledge into three canonical classes.

### 1.1 Foundational Knowledge

Foundational Knowledge contains durable, broadly accepted financial, economic, market-structure, statistical, and model-risk concepts required to reason about investments safely.

Minimum domains include, where relevant to supported markets:

- asset classes: equities, fixed income, foreign exchange, commodities, cryptoassets, and derivatives;
- portfolio construction and diversification;
- capital allocation and risk budgeting;
- factor investing;
- momentum, trend, mean reversion, carry, value, quality, and other researchable return drivers;
- volatility, correlation, covariance, drawdown, tail risk, and stress behavior;
- liquidity and market-capacity constraints;
- market microstructure, order books, spread, slippage, market impact, and execution costs;
- derivatives, leverage, margin, convexity, hedging, and liquidation risk where applicable;
- macroeconomics, rates, inflation, monetary conditions, growth, liquidity, and regime change;
- accounting, financial statements, valuation, and corporate fundamentals where applicable;
- behavioral finance and crowding;
- statistics, probability, uncertainty, calibration, causal reasoning, and hypothesis testing;
- backtest bias, look-ahead bias, survivorship bias, data snooping, multiple testing, and overfitting;
- model risk, distribution shift, data leakage, and uncertainty management;
- transaction costs, taxes/fees where applicable, exchange rules, and operational constraints.

Foundational Knowledge is versioned and reviewable. It may evolve when finance, regulation, evidence, or supported markets evolve, but it is not mutated merely because a recent strategy made or lost money.

### 1.2 Market Knowledge

Market Knowledge contains time-sensitive facts and context that can change independently of NUSA code or learned experience.

Examples include:

- current and historical market data;
- exchange and venue rules;
- instrument specifications;
- corporate actions;
- current macro releases and revisions;
- regulatory or tax rules where adopted;
- news and event information;
- liquidity conditions;
- market structure changes;
- supported broker/exchange capabilities.

Market Knowledge must carry time semantics and provenance. The system must distinguish at least event time, received time, and model-available time where relevant.

### 1.3 Learned Knowledge

Learned Knowledge contains conclusions generated by NUSA research, attribution, simulation, PAPER operation, approved production observations, and controlled continual learning.

Examples include:

- regime-conditioned strategy behavior;
- empirical execution-cost estimates;
- observed model weaknesses;
- strategy interaction and correlation behavior;
- post-trade attribution;
- failure patterns;
- calibrated uncertainty estimates;
- research hypotheses supported by evidence.

Learned Knowledge is never considered permanent truth merely because the system observed it. It must remain challengeable, versioned, attributable, and reversible.

## 2. Knowledge Precedence and Conflict Rules

Knowledge classes have different authority.

1. Safety Constitution and hard policy remain above investment knowledge.
2. Verified market facts override stale market beliefs.
3. Learned Knowledge may refine application of Foundational Knowledge but may not silently invalidate financial or model-risk invariants.
4. Contradictory or stale critical knowledge must become unresolved rather than being guessed into consistency.
5. `UNKNOWN != SAFE` applies to investment knowledge when uncertainty can increase trading risk.

When evidence conflicts, NUSA must expose the conflict, its provenance, and the affected decisions rather than hiding it behind a single confidence score.

## 3. Financial Reasoning Contract

Investment-capable AI components must reason using explicit financial constraints rather than raw language-model confidence alone.

Where applicable, a decision should account for:

- expected return distribution rather than point return only;
- downside and tail risk;
- uncertainty and confidence calibration;
- liquidity and capacity;
- concentration and correlation;
- leverage and margin effects;
- transaction costs, spread, slippage, and market impact;
- time horizon;
- regime dependence;
- opportunity cost;
- data quality and freshness;
- operational reliability;
- policy and risk limits.

No investment AI may treat high linguistic confidence as equivalent to high expected economic value or low financial risk.

## 4. Research-to-Execution Boundary

Knowing an investment concept is not authorization to trade it.

New investment knowledge or a generated hypothesis must follow the applicable validation lifecycle before it can influence production-capable behavior:

`Knowledge -> Hypothesis -> Candidate -> Historical/Point-in-Time Evaluation -> Walk-Forward/Out-of-Sample -> Stress/Adversarial Validation -> Shadow -> PAPER/Canary -> Deployment Gate -> Approved Use`

The exact stages may vary by capability, but evidence-based promotion and independent safety authority may not be bypassed.

## 5. Temporal Integrity

Investment reasoning must preserve what was knowable at decision time.

Backtests, replay, research, and model evaluation must not expose future revisions, future corporate actions, later news, revised macro values, future instrument membership, or other information unavailable at the simulated time.

Point-in-time correctness is a financial-model-risk requirement, not an optional analytics feature.

## 6. Provenance Contract

A material investment knowledge item should be traceable, where technically feasible, to:

- knowledge class;
- source or producer;
- source timestamp;
- event time / received time / model-available time where applicable;
- dataset or artifact version;
- transformation or feature version;
- model / agent / prompt / tool version where AI-derived;
- confidence and uncertainty;
- evidence references;
- validation status;
- superseded or invalidated state.

Unsupported claims must not silently become durable investment knowledge.

## 7. Learned-Knowledge Safety Boundary

Continual learning may improve forecasts, execution, portfolio construction, strategy selection, and other investment capabilities, but it may not autonomously:

- relax hard capital limits;
- bypass the Risk Governor;
- bypass the Deployment Gate;
- enable LIVE mutation;
- redefine immutable safety policy;
- erase adverse evidence;
- convert unverified observations into foundational truth;
- promote its own learned policy without independent validation.

`Learning != Authority`

## 8. Anti-Overfitting and Anti-Contamination Rules

NUSA must defend the knowledge system itself against overfitting.

Required controls include, where applicable:

- held-out evaluation data;
- benchmark isolation;
- point-in-time datasets;
- leakage detection;
- repeated-testing awareness;
- provenance of generated research data;
- separation of candidate generation from final evaluation authority;
- protection against prompt, retrieval, or data poisoning;
- retention of representative historical regimes and crisis periods.

A repeatedly observed result in one regime is not automatically general financial knowledge.

## 9. Knowledge Versioning and Replacement

Investment knowledge must evolve without coupling NUSA to a specific model or provider.

Knowledge stores, retrieval methods, embeddings, models, and agents may be replaced behind versioned capability contracts. The semantic meaning and provenance requirements of canonical investment knowledge must survive provider replacement.

A new AI provider must be able to recover the same governed knowledge classes and financial reasoning constraints from repository and runtime contracts without relying on private conversation memory.

## 10. Investment Knowledge Capability Boundary

The architecture should expose investment knowledge through replaceable capabilities rather than through ad-hoc prompt text spread across agents.

Target capability families include:

- `FOUNDATIONAL_INVESTMENT_KNOWLEDGE`;
- `MARKET_KNOWLEDGE`;
- `FINANCIAL_REASONING`;
- `TEMPORAL_INTEGRITY`;
- `KNOWLEDGE_PROVENANCE`;
- `LEARNED_INVESTMENT_KNOWLEDGE`.

Implementations may evolve independently if they preserve their versioned contracts and safety requirements.

## 11. User-Facing Simplicity

The internal sophistication of NUSA investment knowledge must not force the user to become a quantitative engineer.

Default UI should explain investment conclusions in actionable language, with progressive disclosure for advanced evidence, assumptions, uncertainty, model versions, and financial reasoning.

Internal complexity must not leak into user complexity.

## 12. Architecture Compliance Questions

Any significant Work Order that creates or modifies investment intelligence should answer:

1. Which knowledge class does the change consume or produce?
2. Is the knowledge source versioned and attributable?
3. Is point-in-time correctness preserved where relevant?
4. Can Learned Knowledge be challenged, invalidated, and rolled back?
5. Does the implementation distinguish financial knowledge from trading authority?
6. Are transaction costs, liquidity, uncertainty, and risk incorporated where economically relevant?
7. Does the change introduce hidden model/provider coupling?
8. Can another capable AI recover the same financial constraints from repository/runtime contracts?
9. Does the change preserve Risk Governor and Deployment Gate authority?
10. Is user-facing complexity kept proportional to the user's task rather than the internal architecture?

## Definition of Done

NUSA has a valid Investment Knowledge Layer only when financial reasoning is governed, versioned, provenance-aware, temporally correct, independently verifiable where necessary, safe to evolve, and separable from autonomous trading authority.
