# History mining on years of public Upbit history (design, 2026-11-23; authority_impact: none)

Owner question (2026-11-23): "과거 차트로 하면 안되나". Owner decision (2026-11-23): option **H2** below, and the single look is spent on historical data first. This document fixes **which history may be used for what** before any value is fetched, stored or analysed. It extends `docs/RESEARCH_HISTORY_MINING_DESIGN_2026-11-20.md` (sections 3, 4, 9) and changes no gate, threshold, limit, capital, order switch or authority. It was revised after an independent review (two P1 and several P2 points, section 8).

## 1. Why, and what it cannot do
The host windows of draft PR #3246 give about 18 days of discovery and 22 days of hold-back on four correlated markets. Upbit's public candle API serves years of 60-minute candles, so the look can use about 16 months instead of 3 weeks of hold-back hourly decisions. This lowers the noise of the test; it does **not** make the markets independent (four correlated markets are far fewer than four independent samples) and it does **not** give a second regime: see section 6.

## 3. Decision and split (H2)
Measured with timestamps and counts only (nothing was printed or analysed): Upbit serves 60-minute candles at least back to 2021-12 for BTC, ETH, XRP, SOL and NEAR; first daily candles (listing): BTC, ETH, XRP 2017-09-25, SOL 2021-10-15, NEAR 2021-12-15.
- **Discovery:** 60m candles of BTC, ETH, SOL, XRP, NEAR, 2021-12-20T00:00Z to 2023-06-30T00:00Z.
- **Purge:** at least 96 hours (the existing rule); the plan leaves five days.
- **Hold-back (the one look):** 2023-07-05T00:00Z to **2024-10-31T23:00Z** (about 16 months), BTC, ETH, SOL, NEAR; XRP is discovery-only. It ends before the possible 4h-path span (2024-11-09) and about 2.5 months before the earliest hourly bar any study read.
- The look needs closes up to 2024-11-01T23:00Z for trade exits (the loader reads 24 hours past the window end): the fetch covers it.
- **H1** (unseen markets as hold-back) is not chosen; as an extra check after the look it is not independent (same period, correlated markets).

## 4. Fetch, storage and integrity rules (fixed before any fetch)
1. Commit this design and the fetch script before fetching. Fetching, hashing, storing and automatic sanity checks print no price and are not analysis; a person or a script that analyses the hold-back values before the freeze record would be.
2. **Frozen files stay out of the repository; only a manifest of SHA-256 hashes, row counts and first/last timestamps is committed.** Closed bars are immutable, so a later fetch must reproduce the hashes (as `scripts/fetch-futures-history.js` does). This keeps the hold-back values out of every reviewer's diff.
3. **Hole policy (pre-registered):** Upbit omits a 60m candle in an hour without a trade (a real 4-hour BTC hole exists in 2026-07; NEAR is thin in 2021-2022). Hole hours are stored as absent and never filled. The fetch aborts only on duplicates, disorder, an unclosed bar, an HTTP error or a market whose missing hours exceed 10 percent of its listed span inside a window; the missing share per market and window is part of the manifest.
4. **Timestamp convention check on already-read days only:** compare the committed `docs/research-data/2026-10-08-upbit-1m-closes.json` (2026-10-05 .. 2026-10-08) with the committed 60m files (2025-01-16 .. 2026-10-12) at the hours that overlap; report only the count of agreeing and disagreeing hours. The check must not use host data from 2026-10-07 onward that PR #3246 windows or the forward claims rely on. The collected-store rule is already visible in code: `researchCandleBackfill.ts` stores `close_time_ms = start + 60,000` and `close = trade_price`; the 60m mapping is: a 60m candle starting at `s` becomes the hourly close row at `s + 1 hour`.
5. **What differs from the host store:** the host's hourly row at `t` exists only if the last minute had a trade, so signals that need 25 or 73 consecutive hourly closes lose hours; a 60m candle exists whenever any trade occurred in the hour and its close is the last trade of the hour, possibly earlier than the minute before `t`. The historical series therefore has fewer holes and slightly staler closes in illiquid hours (NEAR early, XRP) than the live store will have in forward use. Per-market usable hours are printed and read before the look.

## 5. Fit with the tool, and what must change before the freeze record
`scripts/run-history-mining.js` reads `research_closed_candles` rows with `interval_ms = 60000` and `close_time_ms % 3,600,000 == 0` through `node:sqlite` (Node 22+), with `--db`, the windows and the cut. A builder writes a temporary SQLite file from the frozen 60m files: schema with `interval_ms = 60000`, a non-null `checksum`, a nullable `volume`, one row per hourly close. A local run needs `NUSA_SOURCE_COMMIT_SHA` set, otherwise the freeze record carries no source commit. Because design section 9.2 changes the script, the run is **not** "unchanged": those changes land first (reviewed), then the freeze record binds the new source commit.
Changes required before any freeze record (they change the pass rule or what is reported):
1. **Excess over an unconditional baseline is part of the frozen pass rule.** In a trending sample a long-only rule passes on drift alone, and the bootstrap null is centred on zero net mean. The look therefore also computes, for each shortlisted rule, the mean of entering at every usable hour with the same hold, and the rule passes only if its excess over that baseline is above 0 with a block-bootstrap test at the Bonferroni level, in addition to the existing net, stress and trade-count conditions.
2. Per-quarter and per-market sign table of net and of the excess (descriptive), so a NEAR-only pass or a single-quarter pass is visible; a block 72 and block 168 sensitivity (descriptive); the break-even cost per shortlisted rule; and a disclosure of entry-rate drift between discovery and hold-back (thresholds come from discovery quantiles; a calmer hold-back can fall under the 50-trade floor and the look reports `LOOK_FAIL` with `testable: false`, which spends the window).
3. Same-close entry (design 9.2 point 2): either enter at the next hourly close or state the assumption in the result.
4. Per-market usable hours in the discover line (design 9.2 point 3).

## 6. Limits stated on purpose
- **One regime.** The repository's own labels (`docs/RESEARCH_NOTE_2026-10-13_OHLCV_CATALOG.md`) mark every daily window from 2022-12-03 to 2025-03-17 as UP, so the hold-back (2023-07 .. 2024-10) is one rising regime; only the discovery window contains a DOWN stretch (2022). The split order (down then up) was chosen knowing those labels, and the boundary 2023-06-30 equals a labelled window start. A rule that passes the excess-over-baseline test in this sample is not shown to work in a falling market. Dip-buy rules found in a falling discovery sample may pass a long-only look on drift alone: this is why the excess test is part of the frozen rule.
- **Survivorship and selection of NEAR.** NEAR entered the research set because it is liquid today (45-day traded value, 2026-11-22). Using it in a 2023-2024 hold-back conditions on survival and later outcome, a look-ahead in the universe, not in prices. SOL has the same issue to a lesser degree; delisted markets are absent. The claim is only "among markets that are large today", and the per-market table shows whether a pass depends on NEAR. DOGE (listed 2021-02, full hourly history) was dropped only because of host collection holes that do not exist in the public history; the blind list is a constant in the tool, changing it is a reviewed code change.
- **Costs.** The fee and the 10 bp / 30 bp reading apply; spreads in 2022-2024 were probably wider, so applying today's costs makes old results **optimistic**, not conservative. The break-even cost per rule is reported.
- Past regimes differ from now; no order book or trade tick history exists on the public API; this route covers candle rules only; the effective sample is far smaller than the hourly count suggests.

## 7. Confirmation
A candidate that passes the historical look is a hypothesis, never evidence of profit and never an order permit. Its confirmation is a **fresh pre-registration on a future span** (design section 4 step 5). The 45 days the host collected are past data, partly read (section 2); they may be used only descriptively. Draft PR #3246 (host windows of 2026-10-08 .. 2026-11-19) is **dropped**: its config would make the host run `discover` on the span that the forward and descriptive claims rely on. Its text stays in the history of that PR; it must not ship.
